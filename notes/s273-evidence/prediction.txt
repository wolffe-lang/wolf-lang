Where the bytes go: NOT the key storage (16 B a key, `keys`/`list` flat), NOT the map's growth (`setonly`
and `rset` linear: 79 B an entry at 100k = 16 B key + 24 B entry + the doubling's abandoned buffers), NOT
the interpolation (16 B a probe key, `rindex` − `rset` = N × 16). Every byte of the blow-up is
wolf-std's `map.get` body, `for pair in m.pairs() { if cmp.Eq.eq(pair.0, k) { return pair.1 } }`:
`pairs()` copies the whole entry buffer into a fresh `List[(K, V)]` on every call
(`__wolf_rt_map_pairs`), in the caller's ambient region, and nothing in a region is freed before the
region ends — for `main` that is the process root, so N gets retain N² × 24 B. The issue's "each `set`
copying the map" is wrong by the ledger: `set` is `m[k] = copy v` and is linear. The checked machine
does the same at its own unit (16 B a slot, 2 slots an entry: 16,047,780 B at 1k) and also keeps every
minted list in its arena for the run (276 MB resident at 1k).

## The changes, and what each will show

1. (#972) A `for pat in m.pairs() { body }` whose receiver `m` is a bare local or parameter of type
   `Map[K, V]` that the body never names is a VIEW of the map's own entries: no list is minted, nothing
   is charged. Native and release lower it as the counted List loop over the map header (the header's
   first five words are a ListHdr, the entry stride is the tuple's list stride); the checked machine
   walks a host-side snapshot it does not retain. Any other `pairs()` (bound by `let`, an argument, a
   body that names the map) materializes exactly as today. One predicate, in wolf_mem, decides for
   both machines.
   - native/release `region` mode = `rindex`'s ledger exactly: 81,040 B at 1k, 1,106,320 B at 10k,
     9,491,344 B at 100k (from 24,129,040 / 2,401,586,320 / killed).
   - native/release `issue` peak: 1k ≈ 1.8 MB, 10k ≈ 2.7 MB (from 2,356 MB; the C bar 2.2 MB, so ≈ 1.2×,
     inside the 4× target), 100k ≈ 10.2 MB and completing (the scan stays O(N) a get, so it is slow:
     tens of seconds, under the 120 s guard).
   - per-entry cost, 10k → 100k slope: native ≈ 87 B of peak (C: 69 B), ledger 93.2 B; before: N × 24 B
     an entry (240 KB an entry at 10k), quadratic.
   - checked: `region` mode at 1k prints 47,780 (= `rindex`, from 16,047,780); `issue` at 1k ≈ 13 MB
     (from 276 MB). At 10k the checked machine stops on its STEP budget (`unsupported`, 50 M loop turns
     of the generic scan against 20 M steps) with its resident set flat (≈ 15 MB), not killed.
2. (#943) The checked machine keeps, per list, the number of slots it has charged; a `push` charges a
   slot (budget and region ledger) only past that high-water mark — the storage `[type.list.reset]`
   says the next pushes reuse. The same for a `Map`'s entries after `clear`/`remove`.
   - w943: checked prints before=128 after=128 delta=0 (from 12928 / 12800); native/release unchanged.
   - variants: checked reset=0 clear=0 pop=0 grow=128, the native answer.
3. Every existing corpus entry keeps its verdict and stdout (no row prints a ledger after a pairs()
